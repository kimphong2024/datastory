02 · Key findings

Eight things the data kept saying.

Across over sixty sources — government reports, academic studies, news, NGO fieldwork — eight patterns repeated often enough to count as structural, not anecdotal.

These are the findings our concepts need to answer to.

Coordination

Schemes exist, navigation doesn't

Only 38% of caregivers know about the Caregivers Training Grant. More than 70% use no caregiver services at all. The state built a rich support layer; families can't find the door.

The gap is search versus relationship. SupportGoWhere answers questions you already know to ask.
Presence

Together but apart

Four in five socially isolated seniors live with family. Loneliness affects 34% of older Singaporeans, rising to 43% of those living alone. Proximity isn't presence.

The app that wins on loneliness is the one that gently notices, not the one that broadcasts.
Protection

Seniors are the scam frontier

Average loss per senior victim reached S$37,053 in 2025 — the highest of any age group. Government-officer impersonation scams surged 123.6% that year. ScamShield acts alone; families find out after.

Protection requires a family co-pilot, not a solo shield. And dialect. Always dialect.
Language

The dialect gap is chronic

Chinese dialect use at home fell from 81% in 1980 to 12% in 2020 — yet Hokkien, Teochew and Cantonese remain emotionally resonant for the 75+. Ami is the rare exception. Tamil and Malay content is thinner still.

Voice-first and dialect-native is the unlock for the oldest cohorts.
Health

Multimorbidity is the default

80.6% of community seniors 65+ have multiple conditions. Polypharmacy drives roughly a quarter of avoidable A&E visits. HealthHub shows records; it doesn't coordinate.

The real product isn't a record. It's the choreography between four doctors and three pharmacies.
Future

Tomorrow's seniors look different

Total fertility rate is 0.97. 14.4% of ever-married women are childless. The 2046 cohort of 65+ is digitally fluent, smaller-family, often spouse-only or solo. Built-in family caregivers will be scarce.

Design for the coming cohort, not the current one. The user you're optimising for is 45 today.
Infrastructure

Physical is solved. Social isn't.

100% of MRT stations are barrier-free. 223 Active Ageing Centres cover 8 in 10 seniors. Silver Zones and Friendly Streets are rolling out. The physical substrate is world-class. The social matching layer on top of it is missing.

Software's job is to make the physical infrastructure feel alive.
Trust

Gov badging is the moat

Older adults trust tools recommended by MOH. "If MOH recommends an app, I feel it must be legitimate" — this quote repeats across qualitative studies. Third-party apps struggle to earn this on their own.

Win the government endorsement conversation early, or lose it quietly later.

Deep dive · 01

Ubat. The medication co-pilot.

Eight pills. Four doctors. Three pharmacies. Two grandchildren reminding. One app that actually helps.

A · Day in the life

Auntie Rosnah, 72 — a Tuesday

Auntie Rosnah, 72 · Bedok North, 3-room HDB · Type 2 diabetes, hypertension, osteoarthritis · Sees polyclinic GP, heart specialist at NHCS, eye doctor at SNEC · 8 regular meds plus occasional painkiller · Daughter Aisha (44) in Woodlands; son Faizal (38) in KL · Speaks Malay, some English
7:02 am · breakfast
Ubat pings — missing morning metformin

Rosnah opens Ubat. Today's tray shows green ticks for 5 of 6 morning meds; metformin slot is amber. She points the camera at the pill; Ubat confirms it's metformin 500mg and logs it. Voice note in Malay: "Knee a bit stiff today."

Caught a skipped dose before it became a pattern
9:15 am · pharmacy pickup
Refill ready at Bedok polyclinic

Ubat had auto-detected her amlodipine was running low 4 days ago, filed the refill through HealthHub, and routed to Bedok Polyclinic pharmacy. Push notification: "Ready after 9am. Queue ticket A42."

No wasted trip, no emergency out-of-stock
11:40 am · NHCS specialist
Cardiologist prescribes bisoprolol

At the visit, Ubat's interaction radar flashes yellow: the new bisoprolol plus her existing amlodipine raises mild fall risk. She shows the screen; the cardiologist adjusts the dose and notes it. Ubat auto-updates the full med list.

Avoided a 3-month silent drug interaction
2:30 pm · home, resting
Appointment braid flags a clash

Ubat shows Rosnah's next 60 days. The SNEC eye appointment on the 24th clashes with a polyclinic blood test on the 23rd that needs fasting. Ubat suggests swapping the blood test to the 21st — one tap books it.

Stopped a double-journey day and a fasting error
6:45 pm · daughter calls
Aisha checks the co-pilot view

Aisha opens Ubat's caregiver view from Woodlands: today's adherence, the new bisoprolol, the knee comment, upcoming appointments, Tier-1 refill cost next month (S$4.30). She voice-notes Rosnah in Malay: "Mak, let me come with you to SNEC next week."

Remote care without remote parenting
10:10 pm · bedtime
A&E QR prepped for the drawer

Ubat prints a fresh QR card — meds, allergies, ACP status, ICE contact. Rosnah keeps it in her handbag. If she falls tomorrow, the paramedic scans it in seconds.

The unplanned moment, planned for

D · Business model & TAM

Three revenue tracks, healthcare-funded

Addressable base: ~850K seniors with multimorbidity today, ~1.25M by 2046. Realistic active users in 5 years: 100K–250K seniors plus 150K–400K caregivers.

Track · 01

B2B2C with healthcare operators

License to polyclinics, clusters (SingHealth, NHG, NUHS), private GP networks, home-care operators. Value justified by reduced readmissions and adherence uplift.

S$3–8 / user / month
Track · 02

Payer partnerships

CareShield Life supplementary insurers — NTUC Income, Great Eastern, Prudential — pay for adherence data that underwrites better policies.

S$12–30 / user / year
Track · 03

Direct B2C caregiver tier

Premium for multi-sibling coordination, TCM interactions, wearables integration. Core stays free for seniors, forever.

S$6–12 / month
Five-year TAM ceiling

Singapore alone: S$180–300M ARR. Regional expansion to Japan, Hong Kong, Taiwan and KL upper-middle: another 3–5× multiple. Exit archetype: acquisition by a cluster, a regional insurer, or a pharmacy parent.

D · Business model & TAM

Not transactional — structurally different

Addressable base: ~700K aged 60–74 (young-old, time and skill) plus ~150K isolated seniors as recipients. Year 5 realistic active: 80K–150K helpers, 50K–100K recipients. No cash exchange between users, ever. It would kill the mutual-aid dynamic and open scam vectors.

Track · 01

Government contract

MOH, MSF, AIC license Jumpa as the volunteer-matching and social-prescription backbone. Per-AAC or per-town-council pricing.

S$30–80K / AAC / year
Track · 02

Corporate CSR

Companies pay for an employee-volunteering module that plugs staff into Jumpa micro-slots. Strong ESG reporting signal.

S$20–60K / company / year
Track · 03

Grants and foundations

Tsao Foundation, Lien Foundation, Temasek Trust Community Foundation, Tote Board — the initial 3-year runway.

S$2–5M · 3 years
Five-year TAM ceiling

Singapore alone: S$25–40M ARR. Small — but Jumpa's real value is as a user-acquisition engine feeding KinCare, Ubat and Kopi. Users who start with Jumpa are 3–4× more likely to adopt the clinical apps later.

06 · Closing

Three convictions the research will not let go.

Conviction · 01

The biggest opportunity is connective tissue. Singapore has built world-class schemes. The seam between them — between Singpass, HealthHub, AACs, caregivers and family — is where a new app earns its keep.

Conviction · 02

Every senior-facing product must be dual-user by default. Senior and family co-pilot. Senior and MDW. Senior and AAC case manager. The solo self-service mental model has capped adoption at 1 in 5.

Conviction · 03

Design for the coming cohort, not the current one. The 45-year-old today is the 65-year-old of 2046: digitally fluent, smaller-family, often childless. The products that matter in twenty years are being imagined now.

Start · here

Ship Jumpa in one precinct within nine months. Ship Ubat to a polyclinic pilot within twelve. Run them as siblings from day one — shared design system, shared trust model, shared caregiver pattern. The 1 + 1 = 3 lives in the cross-referral between them.
